## Stage 2: Identity and Account Trust Review

Once performance has been verified, Facebook begins evaluating the overall trustworthiness of your account.

### Publishing History

Meta also reviews your posting consistency.

## Stage 3: Community Standards Review

This is one of the most important stages.

Facebook reviews your account's history to determine whether you have violated Community Standards.

Previous violations involving topics such as hate speech, graphic violence, harassment, misinformation, adult content, dangerous organizations, fake accounts, or repeated policy enforcement actions may affect monetisation eligibility.

Even if violations occurred months earlier, they can still influence the final review decision.

For this reason, creators should regularly check their Account Status and resolve any outstanding policy issues whenever possible.

## Stage 4: Original Content Detection

Facebook heavily prioritizes originality.

Its automated systems compare your content against millions of existing videos already available on Facebook and across Meta's platforms.

The system attempts to determine:

- Did you create this content?
- Are you the original publisher?
- Does the video contain meaningful creative additions?
- Has this content already appeared elsewhere?
- Is this simply a copy of another creator's work?

Creators who consistently upload their own original videos generally perform much better during review than those who repost trending clips.

# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

## 1. What are Facebook Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP)?

Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP) are Meta's official rules that determine whether a Facebook Page, Professional Mode profile, Group, or Event can earn money through Facebook monetization tools. Every creator must continuously comply with these policies to keep monetization active.

---

## 2. Why did Facebook rename Monetisation Eligibility Standards (MES)?

Meta replaced the previous Monetisation Eligibility Standards (MES) with Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP) to simplify the monetization framework while combining creator eligibility, policy compliance, authenticity, and content quality into one comprehensive system.

---

## 3. How can I check whether my Facebook Page is eligible for monetization?

You can review your Page's eligibility from the Professional Dashboard or Monetization section inside Facebook. The dashboard displays available monetization tools, policy status, Page Quality, and any restrictions affecting your account.

---

## 4. Why is my Facebook monetization unavailable?

Common reasons include:

- Violating Community Standards
- Violating Content Monetisation Policies
- Copyright issues
- Unoriginal content
- Fake engagement
- Country not eligible
- New Page with insufficient history
- Low-quality content
- Policy enforcement actions

---

## 5. Can I monetize AI-generated content?

Yes, but only if the content provides meaningful original value. Simply generating AI images, voices, or text without human creativity, editing, commentary, or educational purpose may not satisfy Facebook's originality requirements.

---

## 6. Does deleting policy-violating content restore monetization?

Removing violating content may help prevent future enforcement, but deleted posts do not automatically restore monetization. If your Page remains restricted after correcting violations, you may need to submit an appeal through Facebook.

---

## 7. Can reposted videos earn money?

Generally, no.

Facebook prioritizes original content. Reuploaded videos without significant transformation, commentary, education, parody, or creative editing are typically ineligible for monetization.

---

## 8. How long does Facebook monetization review take?

Most reviews are completed within a few days, although complex cases or manual reviews may take longer depending on the type of monetization product and policy checks involved.

---

## 9. Will buying followers help unlock monetization?

No.

Artificial followers, fake engagement, purchased views, or engagement exchanges violate Facebook's Partner Monetisation Policies and may permanently affect monetization eligibility.

---

## 10. Can I appeal a Facebook monetization decision?

Yes.

If you believe your Page was restricted by mistake, you can submit an appeal through the Monetization section or Professional Dashboard after reviewing the reason for the restriction.

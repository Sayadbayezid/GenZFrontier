Facebook Content Monetisation Unlock Feature: Meta Starts Reviewing Creators After 300,000 Views

GenZ Frontier Tech Desk | July 11, 2026

Facebook has officially begun rolling out another major milestone for creators through its Content Monetisation program. Creators who successfully reach 300,000 eligible content views within the required qualification period are now automatically moved into Meta's review stage before receiving an invitation to monetize their content.

The review process has become one of the most discussed topics among Facebook creators in 2026 as thousands of Pages and Professional Mode profiles race toward eligibility. A recent creator dashboard screenshot confirms the milestone has been achieved with 306,514 eligible views, triggering Facebook's internal review system.

Unlike previous monetisation programs that relied heavily on follower counts, Meta's latest Content Monetisation initiative places much greater emphasis on original content performance, authentic audience engagement, policy compliance, and long-term account quality.

For creators building a sustainable income through Facebook, reaching the required view count is only the beginning. Passing Meta's review is now the most important step.

What Happens After Reaching 300,000 Views?

Once a creator crosses the required threshold, Facebook automatically displays a confirmation message similar to:

You reached the goal! We will now review your contents to verify that they meet our policies and get back to you before the end of the review period.

This means creators cannot manually unlock monetisation immediately.

Instead, Meta performs a detailed review that evaluates the overall health of the creator account, published content, audience authenticity, monetisation compliance, and previous policy history.

Only creators that successfully pass this review receive an invitation to activate Facebook Content Monetisation.

What Is Facebook Content Monetisation? Everything Creators Need to Know

Facebook Content Monetisation is Meta's latest creator earnings program that rewards eligible creators for publishing original, engaging, and policy-compliant content on Facebook. Unlike older monetisation programs that focused primarily on followers or individual monetisation tools, this new system evaluates creators based on multiple factors, including content quality, audience engagement, originality, and compliance with Meta's monetisation policies.

The goal is simple: reward creators who consistently produce valuable content while protecting advertisers from low-quality, misleading, or copied material.

In 2026, Meta has continued expanding this system globally, allowing more creators to qualify through performance-based milestones instead of invitation-only programs. This change has made Facebook one of the largest creator platforms for earning revenue from videos, photos, reels, and other public content.

The New 300,000 View Requirement Explained

One of the biggest changes introduced by Facebook's latest monetisation system is the performance milestone.

Instead of immediately allowing creators to earn money, Facebook first asks eligible creators to reach a required amount of content views during a qualification period.

For many creators, this milestone currently appears as:

Reach 300,000 eligible content views before the qualification deadline.

Once the required views are reached, Facebook automatically unlocks the next stage of the process—Meta Review.

It is important to understand that reaching this milestone does not automatically approve monetisation.

Instead, it simply tells Meta:

"This creator has demonstrated enough audience activity. Now it's time to verify whether the account deserves monetisation."

That distinction is extremely important because many creators mistakenly assume that 300,000 views guarantee approval. In reality, it only qualifies the account for review.

During this stage, Facebook examines far more than just view numbers.

The platform analyzes overall account quality, publishing history, audience authenticity, copyright compliance, policy violations, engagement quality, and monetisation eligibility.

Why Facebook Introduced a Review Stage

Many creators wonder why Facebook doesn't simply unlock monetisation immediately after reaching 300,000 views.

The answer is trust.

Meta wants to ensure advertisers appear alongside high-quality content created by genuine creators.

Without a review system, spam accounts, fake engagement networks, stolen videos, AI-generated misinformation, and copyright violations could easily enter the monetisation ecosystem.

To prevent this, Facebook verifies whether creators truly deserve access before allowing them to generate advertising revenue.

This review protects:

  • Advertisers
  • Viewers
  • Legitimate creators
  • Facebook's advertising ecosystem

Ultimately, the review process helps maintain higher-quality content across the platform.

One of the most common misconceptions is believing that views alone determine monetisation approval.

In reality, views are only the entry ticket.

Facebook's decision is based on the overall quality of the creator's account—not just one viral video.

Some creators receive millions of views yet still fail review because they publish reused content, violate policies, or rely on artificial engagement.

How the Facebook Review Process Works

The review process generally follows several important stages. While creators only see a simple "Under Review" message in their dashboard, a significant amount of analysis happens behind the scenes.

Stage 1: Performance Verification

Before anything else, Facebook verifies that your account genuinely reached the required qualification milestone.

During this stage, Meta checks whether your eligible views were earned naturally.

The system analyzes:

  • Whether views came from authentic Facebook users
  • Whether the views occurred during the qualification period
  • Whether suspicious traffic patterns exist
  • Whether engagement appears manipulated
  • Whether the creator attempted to artificially inflate statistics

If abnormal activity is detected, Meta may pause the review or investigate the account further before proceeding.

Stage 2: Identity and Account Trust Review

Once performance has been verified, Facebook begins evaluating the overall trustworthiness of your account.

This review focuses on whether your account represents a genuine creator rather than a spam profile.

Several factors contribute to this trust score.

Account Age

Older accounts generally have stronger trust signals because they demonstrate long-term activity.

Brand-new Pages created solely to chase monetisation may receive additional scrutiny.

Profile Completeness

Facebook checks whether your creator profile appears authentic.

Examples include:

  • Profile photo
  • Cover photo
  • Bio information
  • Contact details
  • Public activity
  • Consistent branding

A complete and professional profile creates stronger credibility.

Publishing History

Meta also reviews your posting consistency.

Creators who regularly publish original content over several months usually receive stronger trust signals than accounts that suddenly upload dozens of videos within a short period.

Consistency matters.

Stage 3: Community Standards Review

This is one of the most important stages.

Facebook reviews your account's history to determine whether you have violated Community Standards.

Previous violations involving topics such as hate speech, graphic violence, harassment, misinformation, adult content, dangerous organizations, fake accounts, or repeated policy enforcement actions may affect monetisation eligibility.

Even if violations occurred months earlier, they can still influence the final review decision.

For this reason, creators should regularly check their Account Status and resolve any outstanding policy issues whenever possible.

Stage 4: Original Content Detection

Facebook heavily prioritizes originality.

Its automated systems compare your content against millions of existing videos already available on Facebook and across Meta's platforms.

The system attempts to determine:

  • Did you create this content?
  • Are you the original publisher?
  • Does the video contain meaningful creative additions?
  • Has this content already appeared elsewhere?
  • Is this simply a copy of another creator's work?

Creators who consistently upload their own original videos generally perform much better during review than those who repost trending clips.

Stage 5: Copyright Analysis

Copyright compliance has become significantly more advanced in recent years.

Facebook's automated detection systems analyze:

  • Video footage
  • Audio tracks
  • Music
  • Visual elements
  • Television clips
  • Movie scenes
  • Sports broadcasts

Even short copyrighted segments can trigger restrictions if they violate Meta's monetisation policies.

Creators should always ensure they own the necessary rights or have transformed copyrighted material with meaningful original commentary where applicable.

Stage 6: Audience Quality Review

One of Meta's biggest priorities is authentic engagement.

Facebook wants advertisers to reach real people—not fake accounts.

During review, Meta examines whether your audience appears genuine.

Signals include:

  • Organic followers
  • Authentic watch time
  • Real comments
  • Natural sharing
  • Genuine audience growth
  • Consistent viewer behavior

Artificial growth services, purchased followers, fake likes, or automated view networks can significantly reduce approval chances.

Stage 7: Content Quality Evaluation

Facebook also evaluates the overall quality of your published content.

The review goes beyond simple video resolution.

Meta analyzes whether your content provides value to viewers.

Higher-quality creators generally demonstrate:

  • Clear visuals
  • Understandable audio
  • Informative storytelling
  • Original editing
  • Consistent branding
  • Viewer-focused content

Pages filled with repetitive, low-effort uploads often struggle during monetisation review.

How Long Does Facebook's Review Take?

One of the most common questions creators ask is:

The answer varies.

Some creators receive approval within several days.

  • Number of accounts currently under review

  • Policy checks
  • Identity verification requirements
  • Regional processing

There is no guaranteed approval date.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. What are Facebook Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP)?

Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP) are Meta's official rules that determine whether a Facebook Page, Professional Mode profile, Group, or Event can earn money through Facebook monetization tools. Every creator must continuously comply with these policies to keep monetization active.

2. Why did Facebook rename Monetisation Eligibility Standards (MES)?

Meta replaced the previous Monetisation Eligibility Standards (MES) with Partner Monetisation Policies (PMP) to simplify the monetization framework while combining creator eligibility, policy compliance, authenticity, and content quality into one comprehensive system.

3. How can I check whether my Facebook Page is eligible for monetization?

You can review your Page's eligibility from the Professional Dashboard or Monetization section inside Facebook. The dashboard displays available monetization tools, policy status, Page Quality, and any restrictions affecting your account.

4. Why is my Facebook monetization unavailable?

Common reasons include:

  • Violating Community Standards
  • Violating Content Monetisation Policies
  • Copyright issues
  • Unoriginal content
  • Fake engagement
  • Country not eligible
  • New Page with insufficient history
  • Low-quality content
  • Policy enforcement actions

5. Can I monetize AI-generated content?

Yes, but only if the content provides meaningful original value. Simply generating AI images, voices, or text without human creativity, editing, commentary, or educational purpose may not satisfy Facebook's originality requirements.

6. Does deleting policy-violating content restore monetization?

Removing violating content may help prevent future enforcement, but deleted posts do not automatically restore monetization. If your Page remains restricted after correcting violations, you may need to submit an appeal through Facebook.

7. Can reposted videos earn money?

Generally, no.

Facebook prioritizes original content. Reuploaded videos without significant transformation, commentary, education, parody, or creative editing are typically ineligible for monetization.

8. How long does Facebook monetization review take?

Most reviews are completed within a few days, although complex cases or manual reviews may take longer depending on the type of monetization product and policy checks involved.

9. Will buying followers help unlock monetization?

No.

Artificial followers, fake engagement, purchased views, or engagement exchanges violate Facebook's Partner Monetisation Policies and may permanently affect monetization eligibility.

10. Can I appeal a Facebook monetization decision?

Yes.

If you believe your Page was restricted by mistake, you can submit an appeal through the Monetization section or Professional Dashboard after reviewing the reason for the restriction.
